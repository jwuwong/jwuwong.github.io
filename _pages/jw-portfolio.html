I am a quantitative social scientist finishing my PhD in Linguistics at Stanford, specializing in speech, human perception, and evaluation. I design and run experiments, build and annotate audio datasets, and analyze human judgment data with statistical and mixed-effects models in Python and R.

My research maps directly onto the challenges of building and evaluating modern audio and language AI: eliciting reliable human preferences, calibrating raters, modeling individual variation, and understanding when human-in-the-loop judgment is required versus when automated metrics suffice.

What I do

Human evaluation & preference elicitation. I design forced-choice (2AFC) and pairwise perception experiments to measure how people judge ambiguous, category-divergent speech—methodologically parallel to the pairwise (CMOS-style) evaluation used to benchmark speech and audio models.
Modeling rater variation. I analyze behavioral judgment data with mixed-effects models to separate systematic signal from individual rater variation—the core problem in aggregating subjective human ratings into reliable evaluation metrics.
Audio data collection & annotation. I collect, segment, and annotate large-scale acoustic-phonetic datasets, quantifying fine-grained vocalic and phonetic variation using rigorous, reproducible workflows.
Experimental & survey design. I turn open-ended questions into measurable, well-controlled studies—hypothesis, design, data collection, evaluation, and iteration.
